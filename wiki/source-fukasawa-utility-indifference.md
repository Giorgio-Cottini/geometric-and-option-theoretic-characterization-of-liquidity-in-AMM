# Liquidity Provision of Utility Indifference Type in Decentralized Exchanges

The paper gives a rigorous mathematical formulation of [[concept-constant-function-market-maker|constant function market makers]] built from a utility-indifference principle. A strictly concave utility function `u` defines the trading rule, and this class contains the [[concept-constant-function-market-maker|constant product market maker]] with [[concept-concentrated-liquidity|concentrated liquidity]] as a special case. The authors derive no-arbitrage conditions and the optimal arbitrage trade against an external liquid market, prove that [[concept-impermanent-loss|impermanent loss]] can be super-hedged by a model-free rebalancing strategy when the external price is continuous, establish well-definedness of arbitrage-free reserve processes under nonzero fees for a Uniswap v3-type architecture, and show that [[concept-loss-versus-rebalancing|loss-versus-rebalancing]] vanishes under a nonzero fee when the external price is continuous. They close by reducing a multi-provider pool to a single representative provider and by stating in what precise sense the Uniswap v3 construction is optimal.

**Authors / venue / year:** Masaaki Fukasawa, Basile Maire, and Marcus Wunsch; arXiv 2502.01931; Digital Finance 2025.

## Key points

- **Utility-indifference construction.** The trading function is `φ(x,y,ξ,η) = u*(x + (1-τH(ξ))ξ, y + (1-τH(η))η)`, where `u*(x,y) = u(x* + x, y* + y)`, `u` is strictly concave, increasing, three-times differentiable, `H` is the Heaviside function, `τ ∈ [0,1)` is the fee coefficient, and `(x*, y*) ≥ (0,0)` are the ranges of liquidity provision. A swap `(ξ,η)` is accepted only if it keeps `u*` unchanged, `u*(x,y) = u*(x + (1-τ)ξ, y + η)` for `ξ > 0` (symmetric rule for `ξ < 0`). This is the utility-indifference principle: the liquidity provider is indifferent, in utility, before and after the trade. `u(x,y) = xy` recovers the constant product market maker; `u(x,y) = x^α y^{1-α}` recovers geometric mean market makers. Setting `x* > 0` or `y* > 0` realizes [[concept-concentrated-liquidity|concentrated liquidity]] on a bounded price range, matching [[concept-uniswap-v3-ticks|Uniswap v3]] with `(x*, y*) = (0,0)` as the unbounded, Uniswap v2-type limit.
- **No-arbitrage argument and optimal arbitrage.** With implicit function `f` defined by `u(x, f(x)) = u(x0, y0)`, the ask and bid prices of the pool are `a(x) = s(x)/(1-τ)` and `b(x) = (1-τ)s(x)`, where `s(x) = -f'(x)`. The pool is arbitrage-free at time `t` if `A_t ≥ S*_t` (whenever `X_t > 0`) and `B_t ≤ S*_t` (whenever `Y_t > 0`), against the external price `S*_t`. If either inequality is violated, the paper computes the arbitrageur's optimal trade size in closed form from a first-order condition (e.g. `1/(1-τ) · s(X_t + ξ*) = S*_t`) and shows that executing it restores the no-arbitrage bounds. This is the [[concept-static-arbitrage|no-arbitrage]] machinery that the rest of the paper builds on.
- **Impermanent loss and its super-hedge.** Under zero fee, the pool value is a Legendre transform `v(p) = inf_x { xp + f*(x) }` (Theorem 1), which is concave in the external price and hence forces impermanent loss relative to buy-and-hold: `Y0 + X0·S*_t − V_t ≥ 0`. Under nonzero fee and a continuous external price, Theorem 4 shows that `V − ∫(X_t + X_t^f) dS*_t` is nondecreasing, i.e. the model-free rebalancing strategy `X + X^f` in the external market super-hedges the loss: `Y0 + X0·S*_T − V_T ≤ ∫₀ᵀ (X0 − X_t − X_t^f) dS*_t`. This extends prior Uniswap v2-only results to concentrated liquidity.
- **Loss-versus-rebalancing (LVR).** Theorem 2: if `X` and `V` are continuous semimartingales and `S*` is a positive continuous semimartingale, then `dV − X dS* = −½ f*''(X) d⟨X⟩ ≤ 0`. Comparing to the frictionless replicating strategy `V̂_T = V0 + ∫ X_t dS*_t`, the shortfall is `V̂_T − V_T = ½ ∫₀ᵀ f*''(X_t) d⟨X⟩_t ≥ 0` under zero fee (this is [[concept-loss-versus-rebalancing|LVR]]), strictly positive by convexity of `f` and nondegeneracy of `X`'s quadratic variation. Under nonzero fee (`τ > 0`), the extended LVR bound in Theorem 4 becomes nonpositive: a positive transaction fee eliminates loss-versus-rebalancing whenever the external price is continuous.
- **Multiple liquidity providers reduce to one representative provider.** Section 6 models `N` liquidity providers as subpools and shows a liquidity taker's swap of size `ξ` is optimally allocated across subpools via inf-convolution, `η(ξ; x^(1),...,x^(N)) = min{ Σᵢ η^(i)(ξ^(i), x^(i)) : Σᵢ ξ^(i) = ξ }`. Solving this by Lagrange multiplier shows the internal price stays common across all active subpools after any trade, meaning the wealth dynamics of a single provider are the same whether she opens her own pool or joins an existing one.
- **The exact answer to "in what sense is the Uniswap v3 construction optimal."** For the constant-product case with concentrated liquidity and a common fee tier across providers, the inf-convolution collapses to a single constant-product pool with aggregated virtual reserves, `(X_t + x*)(Y_t + y*) = k²` where `k = Σⱼ kⱼ`, and each subpool's reserves move in exact proportion `dX_t^(j) = (kⱼ/k) dX_t`, `dY_t^(j) = (kⱼ/k) dY_t`, so fee income accrues to each subpool in the ratio `kⱼ/k` of its liquidity depth. Remark 7 states the answer verbatim: "Constant Function Market Makers with concentrated liquidity and with a common fee tier (such as in Uniswap v3): Their construction is optimal in the sense that the distribution of fee income proportional to the liquidity depth of each liquidity provider follows naturally from the optimal allocation of the amount ξ sent to the LP's subpools." Optimality here is allocative and distributional, not shape-optimal in the sense of minimizing impermanent loss or maximizing a fixed provider's utility: Uniswap v3's rule for splitting a taker's order across differently-ranged, same-fee-tier positions is exactly the one that a taker minimizing the amount of the second currency paid (equivalently, an inf-convolution-optimal router) would choose, and that same allocation is what makes per-provider fee share track liquidity depth.

## Notable claims & data

- Trading function: `φ(x,y,ξ,η) = u*(x + (1-τH(ξ))ξ, y + (1-τH(η))η)`, equation (2).
- Ask/bid infinitesimal exchange ratios: `a(x) = s(x)/(1-τ)`, `b(x) = (1-τ)s(x)`, `s(x) = -f'(x)`, equation (5).
- No-arbitrage: `A_t ≥ S*_t` when `X_t > 0`; `B_t ≤ S*_t` when `Y_t > 0`, equations (6)-(7).
- Pool value as Legendre transform: `v(p) = inf_{x∈Ξ} { xp + f*(x) }`, Theorem 1, equation (10).
- LVR under zero fee: `dV − X dS* = −½ f*''(X) d⟨X⟩ ≤ 0`, Theorem 2.
- LVR shortfall under zero fee: `V̂_T − V_T = ½ ∫₀ᵀ f*''(X_t) d⟨X⟩_t`, positive by convexity of `f`.
- Super-hedge of impermanent loss under nonzero fee, continuous `S*`: `V − ∫₀ᵀ (X_t + X_t^f) dS*_t` nondecreasing, Theorem 4.
- Aggregated constant-product-with-concentrated-liquidity pool from `N` same-fee-tier subpools: `(X_t + x*)(Y_t + y*) = k²`, `k = Σⱼ kⱼ`, `kⱼ = √((X0^(j) + x*^(j))(Y0^(j) + y*^(j)))`, with subpool reserves proportional, `dX_t^(j) = (kⱼ/k) dX_t`.
- The construction is closed-form throughout: the value function is an explicit Legendre transform, the CPMM example gives `f*(x) = L²/(x + x*) − y*` in closed form, the optimal arbitrage size solves an explicit first-order condition, and the multi-provider aggregation solves in closed form via a Lagrange multiplier. No numerical scheme is required anywhere in the paper; every result is derived analytically under the stated regularity conditions (`u` strictly concave, three-times differentiable, boundary conditions at 0 and ∞).

## Connections

- Generalizes the [[concept-constant-function-market-maker|constant function market maker]] framework to a utility-indifference class that contains constant product and geometric mean market makers, with [[concept-concentrated-liquidity|concentrated liquidity]] recovered by a bounded range `(x*, y*)`.
- The [[concept-impermanent-loss|impermanent loss]] super-hedge and the [[concept-loss-versus-rebalancing|LVR]] identity extend the Uniswap-v2-only results of Milionis-Moallemi-Roughgarden and of Fukasawa-Maire-Wunsch's earlier work to concentrated liquidity, paralleling the pricing-and-hedging program in [[source-rtw26-cfmm-liquidity-pricing-hedging]].
- The [[concept-static-arbitrage|no-arbitrage]] bid/ask characterization and the optimal-arbitrage-trade derivation match the arbitrage-theoretic treatment used across this thesis's source set.
- The Remark 7 answer on Uniswap v3 optimality is a direct, precisely-scoped counterpart to the open question this thesis pursues under [[synthesis-optimal-liquidity-shape]] and [[concept-optimal-liquidity-provision]]: it settles allocative/distributional optimality (fee share tracks liquidity depth) but leaves shape optimality (which liquidity profile a rational provider should choose) open.
- The multi-provider inf-convolution reduction, and its explicit match to [[entity-uniswap-v3|Uniswap v3]]'s common-fee-tier architecture, gives a formal justification for treating a v3 pool as a sum of independent, per-tick [[concept-liquidity-profile|liquidity profiles]] rather than as a single monolithic curve.

## Open questions

- Remark 7 answers allocative optimality only. It does not address whether the concentrated-liquidity shape itself (the choice of range and profile) is optimal for a provider's risk-return objective, which is the open problem this thesis direction targets.
- The paper assumes a common fee tier `τ^(i) = τ` across providers in the Uniswap v3 analysis. It does not resolve the general case of multiple fee tiers coexisting, which Uniswap v3 also supports.
- The super-hedge and LVR-vanishing results require a continuous external price process; the paper does not quantify the residual loss when jumps are present beyond the general jump formula in Remark 5.
- No empirical calibration is attempted; all results are theoretical, leaving open how large the allocative-optimality gains are in realized fee data.
